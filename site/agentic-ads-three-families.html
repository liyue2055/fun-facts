Agentic Advertising: Three Families, One Scoreboard

Everyone at Ad Week says "agentic." Almost nobody is paying for the part they're talking about. Here's where the money actually is.

TL;DR

The word "agentic" in advertising means ~12 different things. Sorted into three families — A: AI doing the work, B: AI as a place to run ads, C: AI as the customer — and scored by whether real budgets (disclosed, ~$100M+/yr) have arrived, the picture flips the hype: the obsession, agents buying media from other agents, is still pilots and forecasts, while billions already flow through AI production tools and the platforms' own campaign automation — systems nobody bothers to call "agentic."

The pattern to watch: in every use case with real budgets, one company controls the whole loop — the data, the definition of success, the measurement, and the off switch.

2. The three families at a glance

Family A

AI doing the work

AI performs steps of advertising work people used to do — research, creative, planning, buying, optimization.

◉ Real budgets: production tools + platform automation

Family B

AI as a place to run ads

Brands pay to appear inside or next to an AI product consumers use.

◉ Real budgets: AI Overviews ads, ChatGPT ads

Family C

AI as the customer

An agent researches or buys on a consumer's behalf; brands must persuade it.

◉ Early — "impression is not an impression if seen by no human eye"

4. Why the hype and the money are in different places

The accountability gap

Nobody has settled who takes the fall when a buying agent puts a brand somewhere unsafe — and there's no case of it happening yet. But the warning signs are in:

  • IAB Tech Lab's own reference agents hallucinated ad prices in early tests — "fabricated CPM values when pricing data was missing."
  • DataBeat (June): agentic DSPs entered 86% fewer auctions than conventional DSPs, clearing at $6.13 vs $6.95 CPMs — selectivity with unknown payoff.
  • Platform automation already produced the brand-safety scandals: Adalytics found Google automated campaigns on porn/piracy/sanctioned sites (2023) and Amazon DSP + Google ads on illegal-content hosting (2025). The brands' names were on the screenshots.
  • OpenAI's ad terms leave the remedy to OpenAI: a makegood or credit "in its sole discretion" is "your sole remedy." IAB Europe asked who is accountable for AI-driven spend: of 27 respondents who named someone, 12 said the tool user, 8 weren't sure.

The standards race is unsettled

Buyers and sellers haven't agreed how agents connect: AdCP launched Oct 2025 (Scope3, Yahoo, PubMatic; core stable in v3.2), IAB Tech Lab's broader AAMP named in February — with 13 overlapping business functions between them. Two competing protocols this early means nobody's building production on either yet.

The incentive problem

One practitioner told the author: describing his real, working automation doesn't win budget — describing the jazz hands does. Senior agency people say agentic buying is happening; the people who actually run campaigns say nothing is happening. The author's test for any pitch: who is doing it today, with how much money, and what would have to be true for it to grow beyond a test budget?

5. The pattern to watch

In every use case with real budgets, one company controls the whole loop — the data, the definition of success, the measurement, and the off switch. Meta, Google, Amazon: their systems act autonomously inside their own walls and report their own results. Cross-company agent buying needs those pieces shared across buyer, seller, and measurer — which is exactly what hasn't been solved.

Related signals since the article (Oct 2026):

  • Measurement rails arriving for Family B: DoubleVerify launched attribution for ChatGPT ads (via Rockerbox); IAS joined as a media-quality partner in closed pilot. Independent measurement is what turns an experiment into a budget line.
  • TikTok's ad network opened to US advertisers (~400k third-party apps of inventory via TikTok Ads Manager, IAS-measured) with an agentic push — conversational Shopping Assistant, Agentic Leads — plus an MCP letting marketers plug their own agents into TikTok's ads ecosystem.
  • Family B formats expanding: OpenAI testing sponsored agents; Amazon branded conversations set for Jan 2027 US launch.

6. The Feedmob angle

  • Promo-video production sits in Family A's hottest budget zone. AI production/versioning is one of only two Family A use cases with real disclosed budgets — the work Feedmob already does with AI-generated video is the thing advertisers are demonstrably paying for.
  • Family C is the strategic bet under FeedEra. "AI as the customer" — agents researching and buying on consumers' behalf — is early today (8% of brands have a strategy). Early is another word for cheap land: the companies that build for agent-customers now own the position when the behavior arrives.
  • Watch the accountability answer. Whoever solves "who takes the fall" for agent-driven spend — technically and contractually — unlocks the agent-to-agent buying market. Until then, it stays in pilots.
