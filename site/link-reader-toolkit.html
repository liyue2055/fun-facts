How to Give an AI Agent Eyes on LinkedIn

What "Blockus" (browser-use) actually is, what else exists, and the one-step setup that latches Mica onto walled links.

TL;DR

"Blockus" is browser-use — an open-source Python library where an LLM drives a real Chromium browser. It is one of several agent-browser frameworks (others: Stagehand, Skyvern, Firecrawl, Apify).

For latching Mica onto LinkedIn specifically, no new tool is needed: public LinkedIn articles are already readable (proven today — the Ad Week piece was fetched as text with zero setup), and for logged-in content the same pattern already used for Google and GitHub applies — sign into LinkedIn once in the agent's cloud browser, and every future link opens with that session.

中文摘要：你说的 "Blockus" 就是 browser-use（开源项目，让 LLM 直接操作真实浏览器）。但要让 Mica 能读 LinkedIn 这类链接，不需要装新工具：公开的 LinkedIn 文章已经可以直接读取（今天那篇 Ad Week 文章就是这么读到的）；需要登录的内容，沿用之前 Google/GitHub 的做法——在云端浏览器里登录一次 LinkedIn 即可。

1. The problem, precisely

LinkedIn is hostile to bots by design: aggressive bot detection, login walls on profiles and feeds, rate limits, and legal pressure on scrapers. So "reading a LinkedIn link" splits into two very different jobs:

  • Public content (pulse articles, public posts): often fetchable as plain text, no login needed. This already works today.
  • Logged-in content (feed, profiles behind the wall, comments, messages): requires a real authenticated session. This is the part that needs a "latch."

Plain scraping APIs (Firecrawl's normal scrape mode, Jina Reader) are blocked on linkedin.com — the community's escalation ladder says: don't even try them there.

2. What already works for Mica today

  • Text fetch (browser.open): reads public LinkedIn articles end-to-end. Verified today on the Ad Week / Brownstein pulse article — full text, no login, no extra tooling.
  • Cloud browser tasks: the agent's cloud Chromium already holds working logged-in sessions for Google and GitHub (confirmed Oct 5). Any site reachable in that browser is readable, clickable, and actionable — including login-walled pages.
RECOMMENDED
The one-step latch: sign into LinkedIn once inside the agent's cloud browser (same flow as the Google/GitHub logins). After that, every LinkedIn link you drop in chat opens with your session — articles, posts, profiles, comments. No new service, no API key, no per-page fees. When you want it done, just say "log into LinkedIn in your browser" and take over for the login step.

3. The tool landscape — if you want your own stack

For the team's own development (separate from Mica), these are the serious options, all verified in current community usage:

Also worth knowing: OpenBUA runs an agent inside your everyday browser via a Chrome extension — no re-login needed, since it borrows the session you already have. Same idea as the cloud-browser latch, but on your own machine.

4. Recommended setup

  1. For Mica (immediate, free): LinkedIn login in the cloud browser. Covers articles, posts, profiles, comments — everything your account can see.
  2. For one-off bulk jobs (e.g. "pull 200 profiles"): Apify LinkedIn actor or Firecrawl Agent mode — pay per job, no browser to babysit.
  3. For the team's own agent products: browser-use to prototype, Stagehand when it needs to be deterministic and maintainable.
Two honest caveats. LinkedIn's terms prohibit scraping — reading links with your own logged-in session for research is the normal-use lane; bulk extraction is where accounts get flagged, so keep volume human-like. And LinkedIn changes its markup often: anything DOM-dependent (including agent frameworks) needs maintenance; the logged-in-browser approach degrades most gracefully because a human can always take over.
